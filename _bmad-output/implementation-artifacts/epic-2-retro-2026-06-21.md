## What Went Well

- **Defer-then-capstone arc on the failure taxonomy.** The >255-byte-token mislabel (flagged 2.3) and the brittle message-substring `_classifyParseError` (flagged 2.4) were parked with explicit notes and killed as a class in 2.6: `_classifyParseError` deleted, DRM detection moved from matching the word "encrypt" in a locale-dependent message to reading `META-INF/encryption.xml` structurally via `package:archive`. Typed-exception-by-type (born as `EpubEmptyContentException` in 2.4) generalized across the pipeline.
- **On-device escalation caught the biggest real-world bug (2.4).** Synthetic fixtures were green; the first real Calibre-exported EPUB returned `unreadable` because `readBook` eagerly loads every manifest resource and throws on a stray `calibre_bookmarks.txt` (a large share of real books). The typed-failure design failed *gracefully*; the fix was rewriting to the lazy `openBook`/`getChapters`/`readCover` API. Unit → integration → real-hardware paid off.
- **The real EPUB corpus (Epic 1 prep commitment, Nerya) earned its keep** — it surfaced the Calibre bug that no synthetic test would have.
- **Pure-leaf / impure-shell discipline (AR19) held across six stories** — purity grep clean every story; only `runPipeline` crosses the `compute` boundary, drift + `StreamStore` stay main-isolate. `pipeline.dart` split out in 2.3 to keep the grep clean.
- **Inert-not-dead buttons (2.5)** — Send-to-watch and jump-to-chapter render visibly disabled until Epic 4 wires them; no button that lies about being functional.
- **Test infrastructure compounded** — JSON corpus (2.1) → injectable `compute`-runners + temp dirs (2.3) → synthetic in-memory EPUB builder (2.4) → ugly-EPUB corpus reusing the same builder + helpers (2.6). Cheaper to extend every story.

## What Was Challenging

- **The plugin/platform path is the persistent quality gap.** The share-sheet (`receive_sharing_intent`) forced Gradle JVM-target + `compileSdk` surgery in 2.3 and remains manually-verified-only. It accumulated two deferred UX items — silent-drop while an import is in-flight, and markdown-shared-as-`text/plain` losing paragraph structure. Unit/widget tests structurally cannot reach it; the one corner of the epic without a green automated gate.
- **Unicode kept ambushing us — four of six stories.** ORP byte-index vs char-index (2.2), sanitation codepoint-order (2.1), an OPF unpaired UTF-16 surrogate crashing the SQLite insert *after* a good book persisted (2.4 patch), lone-surrogate-can't-survive-the-zip-roundtrip (2.6). A standing tax on anything touching real text.
- **Tests that didn't actually test.** A complexity cap never exercised (2.2), a tautological null-author assertion and a cover test not proving its branch (2.5). Reviews caught each, but it recurred.
- **`AsyncValue.when` loading-precedence trap (2.5).** A StreamProvider in error reports both `isLoading` and `hasError`; `when` checks loading first, so a real DB read error rendered as a perpetual spinner. Fixed with explicit `hasError`-first precedence — and the lesson propagated: 2.6 held its import-failure message in separate screen state rather than the provider's error arm, citing 2.5 by name.

## Key Insights

1. Classify failures by **structure/type, not message text** — the substring-match approach was locale/version-fragile and is now eliminated.
2. **Real corpus + on-device beats synthetic-green** for the import path; the typed-failure design is what made graceful degradation possible while bugs were still latent.
3. The **platform-channel path is the structural blind spot** of a unit/widget-test strategy — it needs deliberate on-device exercise, the way watchdog-class risk did in Epic 1.
